## 1. Evidence

1. **The hits were not read.** SEL-060 Part A counted, from the dispatched sessions' own logs, every hit recall
   handed since it was turned on: 2,264 hits to 286 sessions in seldon, squiddy, hazbrief-mcp and ai-readiness-kg.
   Sessions opened 328 (14.5%); RESULTs named 376 (16.6%) as used, 136 of them never opened. SEL-055's RESULT ("I
   opened none of the eight recall hits") was the common case, not the exception.
2. **What was read was what the task cites.** A RESULT of a task the task file cites was opened 43.8% and used 52.0%
   (n 354); a decision record it cites 63.2% and 68.4% (n 38). A RESULT it does not cite was opened 2.2% and used
   3.8% (n 1,518), at every shared-term count (2 to 4% opened from 4 terms to 12 or more). Two-thirds of the list
   was that last row.
3. **Lexical Issues were the exception.** Uncited Issues were opened 53.8% (n 184), rising with shared terms: 33% at
   4 to 7, 63% at 8 to 11, 84% at 12 or more.
4. **The served arm added nothing read.** 490 hits carried it; sessions opened 2. It costs about 12 s per graph at
   every registration.
5. **Three repositories got no list at all.** factory, critical-thinking-ai and aacps-ai-policy have no `recall:`
   block (101 dispatched sessions), and nothing writes one: RECALL-002R copied the block by hand into three others.
6. **A replay of the new selection** on seldon's and factory's last thirty dispatched tasks (Part C, section 5 of
   `docs/evidence/sel060/replay.md`): the sessions opened 53 of the 233 new hits against 23 of the 240 they were
   handed in seldon, and 21 of 189 against 20 of 240 in factory.

## 2. Prior art

### External

- **Retrieval judged by what the user does with a result.** Click-through as implicit relevance feedback (Joachims
  2002, "Optimizing search engines using clickthrough data", KDD); a click is relative evidence, biased by rank and
  presentation (Joachims, Granka, Pan, Hembrooke and Gay 2005, SIGIR). Named, not fetched. Adopted for R4: an
  open in the session's log is the click, reported by rank, beside the RESULT's explicit `Recall used:` line.
- **Related but unhelpful context lowers answer accuracy, and more of it lowers it more.** Cuconasu et al. 2024,
  "The Power of Noise" (SIGIR), section 5.1, held and checked in factory `docs/evidence/fac16/claims.md` rows C-5 and
  C-6; its F-7 bounds the claim to related documents (random ones raised accuracy). The same file's C-7 (Amiraz
  2025): a stronger retriever's irrelevant passages are each more distracting. Position matters (Liu et al. 2024,
  "Lost in the Middle", TACL, in seldon-biblio as `liu2024lostmiddle`). Adopted for R5: a weak hit is not free, so
  the list may be shorter, or empty.
- **Recommending a developer's past artifacts from the project's history.** Hipikat (Cubranic and Murphy 2003,
  ICSE; Cubranic, Murphy, Singer and Booth 2005, IEEE TSE) recommends earlier change tasks, bug reports and
  revisions linked to the artifact at hand; ROSE (Zimmermann, Weissgerber, Diehl and Zeller 2004, ICSE, "Mining
  version histories to guide software changes") recommends from what changed together. Named, not fetched; the
  library holds neither. Adopted for R5's second stage: an earlier task's RESULT is chosen because its commits
  changed a file the new task names.
- **Inverse document frequency** (Sparck Jones 1972, "A statistical interpretation of term specificity", J.
  Documentation): a term most documents carry discriminates none. Applied for R5 to citations (a header's `DN-005`
  is in 47% of seldon's task files) and to files (a file most tasks change names no particular history). Named.

## 3. Rulings

- **AD-031-R4. Recall's use is counted from the dispatched sessions' own logs: for every registered repository (`recall.audit.repositories_from`, the portfolio's one list) since its first `recall_listed`, `seldon qc recall-audit` reports the hits each launch header handed, the hits a tool call opened and the hits the RESULT's `Recall used:` line names, by repository, kind, rank, shared terms, arm and whether the task file cites the hit.**
  A tool call opens a hit when its input names it: a task or RESULT file by name or by a glob keeping seven literal
  characters and matching at most three task files; an Issue by its id prefix; a record by its id or register file.
  A Write or Edit is not a read. A design note being read is counted apart. Zero model calls.
- **AD-031-R5. With `recall.selection.mode: touch`, registration chooses hits in this order, each carrying one line saying why: the RESULTs, dead ends and decision records the task cites; the RESULT of the earlier task whose commits changed the files the task names or the notes it cites, most specific first, at most `max_file_hits`; Issues naming those files or cited ids, at most `max_issue_hits`; a kept or superseded task of the same code prefix in the last `dead_end_days` that shares a file or enough terms, with its reason; then lexical hits only of kinds whose `lexical_min_shared_terms` is set and only above it. A citation in more than `max_citing_share` of the repository's task files is the template's and is not a reason; a file more than `max_tasks_per_file` tasks changed is not either. The list may be shorter than `top_n`, or empty; the served arm is not run.**
  `mode: lexical`, or no `selection:` block, is RECALL-001's selection unchanged, so the repositories that copied
  the block keep it until they adopt R6's reference. The values are seldon.yaml's, each with its Part A or Part C
  number beside it. The `why` line is a second, indented line under the hit; the hit line itself is unchanged.
- **AD-031-R6. A `recall:` block may be `ref: <file>#<dotted.key>`, resolved from the primary checkout, with the project's own keys laid over the referenced block's; `seldon project new` writes `recall: {ref: <the kit's seldon.yaml>#recall, served: {enabled: false, ...}}` for a repository that has no `recall:` key, and leaves one that has.**
  The served arm is off in the written block because the kit's `served.graphs` names the kit's siblings, not the
  consumer's, and R5 does not run it. A kit with no `recall:` block offers nothing, and the step is skipped. AD-036
  ADDENDUM 03's by-reference form, applied to recall.
- **AD-031-R7. A change to the selection is replayed before it is adopted: `seldon qc recall-replay` re-runs registration for a repository's latest dispatched tasks, as of each launch, under the recorded (or replayed lexical) list and the configured selection, and reports for each how many hits each gives, how many the session opened and which earlier RESULTs it consulted; one checkpoint row per task, so the same command resumes.**
  Only what had landed in the checkout before the launch is a candidate (a RESULT's modification time), so a
  same-day RESULT written later is not "found". The Part A count is re-run one week after the selection reaches
  main, by a task filed with that date in its premise, to read R5 on hits sessions were actually handed.
